## What Went Well

1. Dashboard stayed read-only. The team avoided moving product edits, stock movement writes, History ownership, or Products data ownership into Dashboard.
2. The epic used focused aggregate and limited preview queries instead of widget-side scans, matching the low-end Android and 3,000-product performance posture.
3. Low-stock behavior matured across features. Story 4.4 review caught that Products needed the same restocking rule as Dashboard, including zero-quantity products while preserving the Out of Stock label.
4. Review feedback consistently became regression coverage rather than one-off patches.
5. The final test suite now protects time boundaries, app-shell navigation, retry behavior, friendly error copy, high text scale, and cross-feature consistency.

Amelia (Developer): "Epic 4 did the right kind of Dashboard work: query-focused, read-only, and backed by tests before it became a dumping ground."

Alice (Product Owner): "The value is clear. A store owner can open the app and immediately know what needs attention."

Dana (QA Engineer): "The strongest move was turning every review finding into a test. That is why the Dashboard now feels much harder to regress."

## Challenges And Growth Areas

1. Time behavior needed extra care. Local-day stock changes, UTC storage, DST-safe boundaries, local-midnight refresh, and non-skipping timezone tests all required repeated refinement.
2. Cross-feature consistency was easy to miss. Dashboard and Products initially had a low-stock rule mismatch until review forced one shared interpretation.
3. Dashboard stream expansion affected app-level tests. Each new provider required deterministic overrides in launch, router, and smoke tests to avoid hanging or lifecycle-sensitive tests.
4. Empty-state logic became more nuanced once recent activity could exist even when product summary counts were zero.
5. Responsive layout and enlarged text needed repeated hardening as summary cards, preview rows, actions, and notes accumulated on one mobile screen.

Winston (System Architect): "That is not a failure of the design. It means Dashboard is doing its job as a read model over Products, Stock, and History."

## Key Insights

1. Dashboard should stay a read model. Its power comes from summarizing Products, Stock, and History without owning their mutations.
2. Shared business rules need explicit cross-feature tests. Low stock, out of stock, and restocking rules should be protected wherever multiple screens interpret them.
3. Time logic deserves deterministic tests. UTC storage plus local user-visible days is a real product requirement, not an implementation detail.
4. App-shell tests need provider override discipline as feature streams grow.
5. Empty states should consider the whole user story, not only one count. Recent activity can still be useful when current inventory is empty.
